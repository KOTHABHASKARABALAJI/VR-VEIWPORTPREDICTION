## 1. System Overview & Purpose

Module 3 implements the **Viewport-Aware Tile Quality Allocation Strategy** for the canonical 4 rows $\times$ 6 columns (24 tiles) Equirectangular Projection (ERP) video canvas ($3840 \times 1920$ resolution). 

In 360° video streaming, the user only observes a subset of the spherical canvas at any instant (typically 8–10 tiles for a 100° $\times$ 100° Field of View). Rather than distributing network bandwidth uniformly across all 24 tiles (the Module 2 non-adaptive baseline), the viewport-aware allocator exploits the spatial importance distribution generated by our predicted viewport orientation. It prioritizes high quality tiers ($Q3, Q4$) for tiles within the user's line of sight, while maintaining low-overhead background quality ($Q1$) for occluded tiles, all while strictly satisfying a downlink bandwidth budget $B_{\text{budget}}$.

## 2. Mathematical Formulation & Greedy Algorithm

### 2.1 Problem Formulation
Given:
- A set of $N = 24$ spatial tiles $\mathcal{T} = \{0, 1, \dots, 23\}$.
- Normalized viewport importance weights $\mathbf{w} = [w_0, w_1, \dots, w_{23}]$ satisfying $w_i \ge 0$ and $\sum_{i=0}^{23} w_i = 1.0$, derived from ray-intersection sampling in `ViewportMapper`.
- Four discrete quality tiers $\mathcal{Q} = \{Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4\}$ with simulated bitrates $R(q) \in \{100, 300, 600, 1000\}\text{ kbps}$ and concave diminishing numerical utilities $U(q) \in \{1.00, 2.00, 2.80, 3.40\}$.
- Available downlink bandwidth budget $B_{\text{budget}} \ge 2,400\text{ kbps}$.

The objective is to find a per-tile assignment vector $\mathbf{q} = [q_0, q_1, \dots, q_{23}] \in \mathcal{Q}^{24}$ that maximizes the viewport-weighted utility subject to the total canvas bitrate constraint:
$$\max_{\mathbf{q}} \quad U_{\text{weighted}}(\mathbf{q}) = \sum_{i=0}^{23} w_i \cdot U(q_i)$$
$$\text{subject to} \quad \sum_{i=0}^{23} R(q_i) \le B_{\text{budget}}$$
$$q_i \in \{Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4\} \quad \forall i \in \{0, 1, \dots, 23\}$$

### 2.2 Discrete Greedy Marginal Optimization
Because the utility increments exhibit diminishing marginal returns ($\Delta U / \Delta R$ is non-increasing across successive tiers), this discrete resource allocation problem can be solved with a deterministic greedy algorithm:

1. **Baseline Initialization:**
   Assign baseline tier $Q1$ (Low, 100 kbps) to all 24 tiles:
   $$q_i \leftarrow Q1 \quad \forall i \in \{0, \dots, 23\}$$
   $$\text{Initial Bitrate } R_{\text{total}} = 24 \times 100 = 2,400\text{ kbps}$$

2. **Candidate Marginal Evaluation:**
   For each tile $i \in \{0, \dots, 23\}$ currently at tier $q_i < Q4$, let $q_i^+$ denote its next higher tier ($Q1 \to Q2$, $Q2 \to Q3$, or $Q3 \to Q4$).
   The incremental bitrate and utility gains are:
   $$\Delta R_i = R(q_i^+) - R(q_i)$$
   $$\Delta U_i = U(q_i^+) - U(q_i)$$
   The marginal score of upgrading tile $i$ is:
   $$\text{score}_i = \frac{w_i \cdot \Delta U_i}{\Delta R_i}$$

3. **Marginal Rate Schedule per Tier Transition:**
   | Transition | $\Delta R$ (kbps) | $\Delta U$ | Marginal Efficiency ($\Delta U / \Delta R$) | Marginal Score Formulation |
   | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
   | $Q1 \to Q2$ | $+200$ kbps | $+1.00$ | $0.00500\text{ util/kbps}$ | $w_i \times 0.00500$ |
   | $Q2 \to Q3$ | $+300$ kbps | $+0.80$ | $0.00267\text{ util/kbps}$ | $w_i \times 0.00267$ |
   | $Q3 \to Q4$ | $+400$ kbps | $+0.60$ | $0.00150\text{ util/kbps}$ | $w_i \times 0.00150$ |

4. **Feasibility Filtering & Greedy Selection:**
   Filter all candidate upgrades that fit within the remaining bandwidth headroom:
   $$\mathcal{C}_{\text{feasible}} = \left\{ i \in \{0, \dots, 23\} \;\middle|\; q_i \ne Q4 \;\text{ and }\; R_{\text{total}} + \Delta R_i \le B_{\text{budget}} \right\}$$
   If $\mathcal{C}_{\text{feasible}} = \emptyset$, terminate the algorithm.

5. **Deterministic Tie-Breaking:**
   Select the candidate $i^*$ with the highest score:
   $$i^* = \arg\max_{i \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{feasible}}} \text{score}_i$$
   When multiple candidates share the exact same score within numerical precision ($|\text{score}_a - \text{score}_b| \le 10^{-12}$), ties are broken deterministically by selecting the **lowest tile ID** ($\min \text{tile\_id}$).

6. **Iterate:**
   Apply the upgrade: $q_{i^*} \leftarrow q_{i^*}^+$, update $R_{\text{total}} \leftarrow R_{\text{total}} + \Delta R_{i^*}$, and repeat from Step 2 until no feasible upgrade remains.

## 3. Strict Validation & Safety Rules

1. **Complete 24-Tile Weights:**
   - Must provide exactly 24 weight entries corresponding to tile IDs $0 \dots 23$.
   - Rejects boolean keys/values, non-numeric types, negative weights, NaNs, and infinite values.
   - Enforces $\sum_{i=0}^{23} w_i = 1.0 \pm 10^{-4}$.
   - Convenience helper `expand_weights_to_grid(sparse_weights)` maps sparse `ViewportIntersection` weights to the full 24-tile grid.
2. **Sub-2,400 kbps Budget Rejection:**
   - Transmitting 24 tiles at lowest tier Q1 requires at least $2,400\text{ kbps}$.
   - Any budget strictly below 2,400 kbps raises `InsufficientBandwidthError` to guarantee no silent over-allocation.
3. **Budget Invariance:**
   - Total simulated bitrate strictly satisfies $R_{\text{total}} \le B_{\text{budget}}$ across all configurations and sweeps.
4. **Zero-Weight Tile Policy:**
   - Tiles with $w_i = 0.0$ are accepted and handled safely without division by zero.
   - By default (`allow_zero_weight_upgrades=True`), surplus bandwidth remaining after all positive-weight tiles reach Q4 can be used to upgrade background tiles (broken deterministically by tile ID).
   - If `allow_zero_weight_upgrades=False`, zero-weight tiles strictly remain at Q1 to conserve bandwidth.

### Key Analytical Insights:
1. **Elimination of Wasted Bandwidth:** At 5,000 kbps, uniform streaming is constrained to Q1 (2,400 kbps), wasting 2,600 kbps of network capacity. Viewport-aware streaming utilizes the entire 5,000 kbps to deliver Q3/Q4 within the viewport, boosting perceived utility from 1.00 to 2.83 (+182.7%).
2. **Quality Concentration:** At 7,200 kbps, uniform streaming spreads bitrate equally (Q2 across all 24 tiles). Viewport-aware streaming concentrates bitrate into the active viewing frustum (Q4 at focal center), raising weighted utility from 2.00 to 3.30 while using 100 kbps less total bandwidth.
3. **Trade-off:** Mean canvas utility across the entire sphere is lower under viewport-aware streaming (e.g. 1.60 vs 2.00 at 7,200 kbps), but weighted viewport utility inside the user's line of sight is drastically higher (3.30 vs 2.00), demonstrating the core thesis of viewport-adaptive streaming.

## 7. Scientific Scope & Disclaimers

> [!NOTE]
> 1. **Illustrative Simulation Model:** Bitrates ($100, 300, 600, 1000\text{ kbps}$) and utilities ($1.00, 2.00, 2.80, 3.40$) are **simulated transmission assumptions**. They do not represent measured video encodings (e.g. FFmpeg H.265/AV1 streams) or hardware decoder bitrates.
> 2. **Unitless Utility Abstraction:** Weighted utility is a mathematical optimization abstraction modeling diminishing marginal visual returns. It does **not** represent objective metrics like PSNR, SSIM, or VMAF, nor subjective Mean Opinion Scores (MOS).
> 3. **Perfect Horizon Assumption:** Viewport-aware allocation assumes head orientation is predicted with lead time $k=5$ frames ($\sim 0.5\text{s}$) by our Day 1 GRU model. Unpredicted rapid head saccades may cause peripheral tiles at Q1 to enter the user's field of view. This boundary risk will be directly mitigated in **Module 4: Neighbourhood-Aware Quality Allocation** via spatial guard-band buffering.
